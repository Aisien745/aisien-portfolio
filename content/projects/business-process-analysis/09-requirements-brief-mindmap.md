## 01. Modeling Objective & Diagnostic Context

During discovery interviews with the Shop Floor Head at **Cherepovetskie Paints**, outdated operating documentation emerged as the primary driver of recurring manufacturing errors. Because legacy SOPs are no longer maintained, procedural updates are passed orally and critical tribal knowledge evaporates whenever experienced operators leave.

To align process modeling with the executive mandate—**modernizing and standardizing regulatory operating documentation**—a structured **Business Requirements Brief** was designed using a five-pillar mind-map framework covering **Goals**, **Business Processes**, **Resources**, **Personnel**, and **Information Systems**.

## 02. Five-Pillar Requirements Brief (Template vs. Populated Scope)

| Management Pillar | Node | Brief Template Question | Populated Client Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Goals** | **Goal 1** | Define core efficiency, quality control, and documentation accuracy objectives. | Increase production efficiency by **15%** through standardized, accurate operating documentation. |
| **1. Goals** | **Goal 2** | Establish concrete targets for reducing shop-floor execution errors. | Reduce manufacturing process errors by **20%** within the next 12 months. |
| **1. Goals** | **Goal 3** | Define deliverables for updating and maintaining SOP currency. | Bring **100%** of regulatory SOPs up to date and guarantee universal access for all shop-floor staff. |
| **2. Business Processes** | **Process 1** | Assess current documentation workflows and systemic deficiencies. | Existing documentation workflows are obsolete, causing recurring errors and institutional knowledge loss. |
| **2. Business Processes** | **Process 2** | Map production stages and isolate recurring error hotspots. | Manufacturing relies on multiple manual handoffs where errors repeatedly occur due to missing instructions. |
| **2. Business Processes** | **Process 3** | Audit how procedural updates are communicated across teams. | Procedural updates are shared informally and orally, generating inconsistencies and compliance drift. |
| **3. Resources** | **Resource 1** | Inventory existing regulatory documents and lifecycle status. | Legacy technical instructions and SOPs require comprehensive audit, revision, and version control. |
| **3. Resources** | **Resource 2** | Identify human subject-matter experts holding critical process knowledge. | Tenured production specialists possess tacit operational knowledge that must be extracted and codified. |
| **3. Resources** | **Resource 3** | Evaluate tools available for document control and production tracking. | Current tooling consists of standalone document repositories disconnected from real-time shop-floor data. |
| **4. Personnel** | **Specialist 1** | Identify key operational leads governing production and documentation. | **Production / Shop Floor Head** — accountable for overarching manufacturing execution and process control. |
| **4. Personnel** | **Specialist 2** | Define roles responsible for quality standards and dissemination. | **Quality Control Manager** — accountable for enforcing product quality and regulatory compliance standards. |
| **4. Personnel** | **Specialist 3** | Address turnover exposure and assign documentation ownership. | **Documentation Specialist** — accountable for maintaining, versioning, and distributing updated SOPs. |
| **5. Information Systems** | **System 1** | Map existing IT systems managing production and document data. | Basic standalone document management system currently used without shop-floor integration. |
| **5. Information Systems** | **System 2** | Specify platform requirements for maintaining updated SOPs. | Integrated digital platform connecting live production workflows with version-controlled SOP records. |
| **5. Information Systems** | **System 3** | Audit internal communication channels used for team updates. | Heavy reliance on email and verbal communication, requiring migration to structured digital distribution. |
