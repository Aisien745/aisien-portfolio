## Data and Facts

### 1. Company Background
- Vernon Stuber founded the SwissOne brand, building on his years of experience in the Austalian market manufacturing congfectionery products that contained no artifical flavours, colours, or vegetables fats.
- Quality of Swiss chocolate was declining beacause companies were using more sugar to lower their costs.
- Company concentrated on quality, sustainability, and reviving the concept and legacy of traditional high-quality Swiss chocolate.
- Differentiation from other companies: more cacao and less sugar.
- In 2021, SwissOne established a sustainable supply chain that was aligned with the Fairtrade Max Havelaar Foundation. This provided SwissOne's cacao farmers with excellent working conditions and fair wages, and it offered traceability and transparency to SwissOne's customers. However, the company was still fairly young and still working to improve its brand awareness.

### 3. The Global Chocolate Industry
- The global chocalate industry generated revenues of approximately $106.1 billion - or 49.1 per cent of the total global candy and chocolate manufacturing market - in 2020
-  Key global players (30 per cent of global market share):
    - Mars Incorporated (US)
    - Ferrero (Italy)
    - Mondelez International (US)
    - Nestle (Switzerland)
    - The Hershey Company (US)
- Despite the entrenched dominance of century-old, highly reputable chocolate brands, the industry still offered room for growth driven by evolving consumer tastes and a rising demand for affordable luxury among the middle classes in emerging markets like Brazil, China, and India.
- 3 main cacao beans:
    - Forastero (Bulk Cacao)
    - Criollo (Fine-grade cacao)
    - Trinitario (Fine-grade cacao)
- Fine-grade cacao composed 5 per cent of global cacao production, but because it offered a complex flavour profile, featuring fruit, floral, and wood notes, customers considered it a premium cacao. Manufacturers fermented, dried, roasted, and either.
- 3 main varieties of chocolate
    - Milk (10-50% cacao powder)
    - Dark (50-90% cacao powder)
    - White (no any cacao)
- Dark chocolate is rich in flavanol antioxidants -> health-conscious customers is shifting preference from milk to dark chocolate
- While sourcing cacao provides vital income to developing countries, it is plagued by severe ethical and sustainability issues, including extreme poverty, deforestation, and widespread child labor. 
- To address growing consumer demands for corporate social responsibility, chocolate manufacturers are increasingly adopting certifications like Fairtrade to ensure ethical practices and fair wages. 
- Although these sustainable sourcing methods raise production costs, they successfully differentiate ethical brands from their competitors in the market.
- Cacao price volatility and crop vulnerabilities create unstable profit margins that disproportionately threaten smaller chocolate manufacturers lacking economies of scale. 
- To mitigate these financial and supply risks, companies often diversify their suppliers and substitute cacao with cheaper, less volatile ingredients like sugar and vegetable fats, ultimately compromising the quality and healthiness of the chocolate.
- Although artisanal chocolate represents only 27% of global consumption alongside standardized industrial manufacturing, the industry is increasingly adopting technologies like blockchain and cacao traceability for quality control, while also introducing product innovations like Callebaut's ruby chocolate.
- Chocolate demand is elastic and highly seasonal, peaking during major winter and spring holidays and dropping in warmer months when consumers favor less heat-sensitive substitutes. 
- To offset this summer decline, brands are leveraging co-branding partnerships for innovative products like frozen desserts, while continuing to rely heavily on supermarkets and hypermarkets, which account for over 40 percent of global sales.

### 4. The Swiss Chocolate Industry

#### History
- Francois-Louis Cailer established Switzerlands's first chocolate factory in 1819.
- Two centuries later, the Cailler chocolate brand remained the oldest chocolate brand in Switzerland.
- 3 developments gave Switzerland a competitive advantage:
    - 1826 - Philippe Suchard invented the first machine that could mix cacao powder and sugar
    - 1875 - Daniel Peter (Cailer's son-in-law) invented milk chocolate; he had discovered that using powdered milk, rather than fresh milk, could prevent the development of mildew.
    - 1879 - Rodolphe Lindt created a machine that created melted chocolate, making it easier to pour and shape into moulds.
- 1908 - Theodor Tobler and his cousin Emil Baumann established Toblerone in Bern (Switzerland)
- They created a unique recipe (consisting of milk chocolate, almonds, honey, and nougat) and triangular shape and
- 1919 - up to 75 per cent of the chocolate that Lindt & Sprüngli produced was exported overseas.
- After industrialization pushed Swiss chocolate producers to rely heavily on overseas exports, global disruptions from the Great Depression and World Wars severely restricted their access to raw materials and international markets.
- In response, the industry strategically pivoted to secure and solidify its presence in the neighboring European markets of Germany, France, and Italy.

#### Competitive Landscape
- 2020 - Swiss chocolate production totalled about 180,000 tons, with sales of around $1.5 billion.
- Switzerland is a leader in chocolate consumption - at 10,4 kg per capita annually.
- Around 34 per cent of Swiss consumers reportedly ate chocolate products more than five times per week, and the most common varieties they consumed were milk chocolate, dark chocolate, and chocolate with nuts. 
- Chocolate bars were the most popular product in the Swiss chocolate market in 2020, accounting for 51.2 per cent of sales.
- Switzerland was also one of the world's top chocolate exporters.
- A total of 70 per cent of Swiss chocolate was exported overseas; the top five export destinations were Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Canada, and the United States.
